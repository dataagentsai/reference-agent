Sixteen people · five groups · one codebase

Working Sessions

Each group takes one area of the harness, goes deep enough to teach it, and then does — runs a working session for everyone else. The point is not five presentations. The point is that by the end, sixteen people can argue about the seams.

Companion to AGENT-HARNESS-EXPLAINED.html, which is the reading. This is the doing.

Section 01

The shape

16people

2weeks

Groups 1 and 3 get four people because they are the hardest — the loop, and distributed waits. Group 5 is smallest but touches everything, so put a strong person there.

Why these five and not the obvious four

The instinct is orchestration · durability · observability · MCP. It is close, but uneven — MCP is a day and observability is a week, and it leaves policy, identity and cost with no home at all. Five groups balances the code and gives each one a sharp identity.

Each group gets a question, not just a topic

From the twelve roles in the explainer. Knowing who you are teaching for changes what you look at.

Section 02

How deep is deep enough

You can draw it from memory, point at the line that enforces it, and name the bug that proves why it is there.

Six questions, identical for all five groups. A group that can answer these is deep enough to teach.

  1. Draw it. Where does control enter, where does it leave?
  2. What one decision does this area own that nothing else may make?
  3. Show the line where that is enforced — not the docstring, the code.
  4. What broke? Find the F-number or the scarred comment. Every area has one.
  5. How is it tested, and what does the test not cover?
  6. Adopt or build — and what stays ours either way?

Question 4 is the one that changes the exercise. This codebase documents its own defects in the comments — F-004, F-006, F-013, F-022, F-023, F-031, F-039, R-017, AACP-0056 and more. Each group finds theirs and explains what the code looked like before. That is the difference between reading code and understanding it.

The five briefs

The deep questions below are hard on purpose. They are all answerable from the code, and none of them is answerable by skimming.

The turn

How one message becomes a reply.

Read

One anchor for group 5

Group 5 is the widest and shallowest — six modules, none large, and the risk is skimming. Give them one concrete artefact: trace the poisoned note in AB-66666 from the tool result, through all five policy positions, to the reply. That single trace touches policy, identity, context fencing and the tool boundary at once.

Section 04

The seams

This is where the learning is. Each seam is a place two groups must agree, and where the interesting arguments happen.

SeamBetweenThe question to settle
The idempotency key1 ↔ 2 ↔ 3Minted in the loop, checked at the boundary, stored in a ledger, sent to the far end. Who owns it?
The checkpoint1 ↔ 3What exactly does a turn save, and why does a half-durable set refuse to start?
Who may approve3 ↔ 5HTTP scope, workflow validator, far-end record — three checks, three different questions. Which is which?
Surface = scopes2 ↔ 5One operation → scope table drives both the model's tool surface and the far end's demand. Trace both.
The span contract4 ↔ everyoneEvery group's code emits spans. Group 4 owns the contract that fails the build. Does each area actually satisfy it?

Group 4 presents last. Their topic is everyone else's code. They get far more out of it once the other four have gone — and they can close by showing each group where their concern appears in a single trace.

Section 05

Running the sessions

The forty minutes

TimeWhatRule
5 minThe claim — one sentence: "this area owns X, and nothing else may decide it"no preamble
10 minRun it livea terminal, not a screenshot
10 minWalk one path through the codereal files, no slides
5 minThe defect — what broke, what it looked like, the fixname the F-number
10 minQuestions, and the handoff to the group you share a seam with—

The two rules that do the work

1 · No slides except the diagram you drew. Not copied from the explainer — drawn. If a group needs slides, they have not understood it yet.

2 · Each group sets three questions for the room, asked at the end of their slot, and anyone may be called on. This is the single change that turns an audience into participants. People listen differently when they might be asked.

Every group produces the same three things

Otherwise you get five presentations of wildly different shapes.

  1. One diagram they drew themselves. Drawing it is where the understanding happens.
  2. One thing they ran live. Every command in the briefs works.
  3. One finding. A question the code does not answer, a comment that no longer matches the code, or a gap. Capped at one, so they hunt rather than list.

Section 06

Facilitator notes

A week before

Everyone reads the two prologues in the explainer — An agentic system is a software system and Who it has to work for. Fifteen minutes, and it stops five groups independently concluding that agents are a new discipline.

The mid-week check

Ask each group for their diagram only. Nothing else. If it is a copy of one from the explainer, they are behind — and you will know four days early rather than in the room.

Where the time will go wrong

GroupRiskWhat to do
3 · DurabilityDeepest by a distance — distributed waits, three idempotency layers, Temporal's modelfour people and a head start
5 · GuardrailsWidest and shallowest; will skim six modulesanchor on the AB-66666 trace
1 · The turnWill spend all their time in loop/ and never reach the routermake them present the router first
4 · WatchingBreadth without depth — six products is a lot of UI to admireone trace, one metric, one firing alert. Nothing else.
2 · Tools & MCPMost self-contained; may finish earlygive them the far end's seven checks as the stretch

What you are really collecting

One finding per group. There are live ones already in the code, waiting to be found:

FindingWhose
InMemorySpanExporter is never cleared, so a long-running process accumulates every spanGroup 4
ApprovalRequested discards a sibling's result mid-batch, so an effect can happen with no record in the transcriptGroups 1 + 3
A content_filter finish is reported as ModelMalformed — a refusal recorded as unreadabilityGroup 1
agent.tool.calls has no truncated label, so AACP-0004's signal cannot be computedGroup 4
Do not hand these out

If a group finds one of these independently, that tells you more about their depth than any presentation will. If a group finds something not on this list, better still.

The one outcome to aim for

Not five people who know their area. Sixteen people who can argue about a seam — because that is the only conversation where design actually happens, and it is the one nobody can have alone.
